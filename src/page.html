A wall, a plank, and a palette in one hand.

A Madonna going up on the outside wall of an old stone building — painted straight onto the render, freehand, from a plank laid across two trestles. No projector, no transfer, no grid. The drawing is made at full size, on the wall, in the light it will live in.

This is the training the rest of the work rests on. Everything in the gallery below — the painted loggias, the grisaille panels, the ceilings — comes out of the same hand doing the same thing.

25 seconds, no sound. The approach at the start runs at speed; the painting is real time.

01 — What he does

A surface is a place to begin, not a thing to cover.

Most of this work is done by hand, on site, in the room it belongs to: mineral pigment and lime plaster, glaze built in layers, gold leaf laid over bole. It is the vocabulary of the Italian workshops, applied to walls, ceilings, doors, panelling and garden objects — in private homes, and increasingly in restaurants and commercial interiors. Marco also restores existing decorative work and murals, his own and other people’s. If you are an interior designer or an architect, these are the terms on a specification.

Trompe-l'œil FR
Painting that reads as architecture. A flat wall becomes a colonnade; a blank ceiling opens onto sky.
Grisaille FR
Monochrome painting in greys and stone tones, used to imitate carved relief and ornament.
Marmorino IT
Burnished lime-and-marble-dust plaster. Depth and a soft sheen that paint cannot reproduce.
Bas-relief FR
Low modelled relief built up in plaster, then painted — ornament you can feel as well as see.
Strié FR
Fine dragged brushwork in glaze, leaving a subtle vertical grain across a whole wall.
Doratura IT
Water and oil gilding — leaf laid, burnished and toned so it sits in the room rather than shouting.
Finto marmo IT
Painted marble and stone: veining drawn by hand to a real specimen, then glazed and polished.
Patina IT
Controlled age. Wash, wear and chalk applied so new work carries a century of weather.
Restauro IT
Repair of existing decorative work — matching an old hand, filling losses, re-integrating damage.

03 — The maker

Trained where the walls were already painted.

Marco Zanardi Lamberti was born in Mantova, a città d'arte in Lombardy, and grew up surrounded by architecture, painting and sculpture. He enrolled at art school in Verona, where he worked mainly in sculpture and his teachers took to calling him Maestro.

The third of four sons of a nobleman, he was expected to become an architect or an engineer. His father did not approve of art school. His mother did — and sent him south to stay with her brother on Ischia, in the bay of Naples, where he could keep studying. He took his degree there in fine arts.

He worked next as a painter-decorator and design consultant in Locarno and Paris. In the 1980s he moved to Los Angeles, which has been home ever since.
